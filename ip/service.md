## Design Notes

- **Typography:** Use same font stack as `index.md`. Service names in bold, descriptions in regular weight for readability.
- **Spacing:** Generous whitespace between service cards — don't cram content. Each service should feel distinct and scannable.
- **Mobile:** Stack all grids on mobile. Ensure service cards are readable on small screens with clear visual hierarchy.
- **Icons:** Optional icon or visual treatment for each service card — use consistent style (outline or filled, not mixed).
- **CTAs:** Each service card should have a clear, action-oriented CTA that matches the service (not generic "Contact Us" for everything).
- **Tone:** Conversational, honest, grounded — no corporate jargon or inflated claims. Focus on outcomes, not features.

## Service Page Best Practices Applied

This spec follows patterns from effective service pages at leading agencies and tech companies:

1. **Outcome-first descriptions** — Each service opens with what the customer gets, not what you do. This follows the pattern from companies like Ramp and Mercury that lead with quantified value. [web:9][web:18]

2. **Scannable structure** — Service cards use consistent formatting (outcome, bullets, CTA) so visitors can quickly compare options. This mirrors the approach at companies like Nitsch Engineering and HITT. [web:18]

3. **Trust signals embedded in flow** — The "Why This Matters" section builds credibility by acknowledging limitations (we don't do everything) — a pattern used by companies like Anthropic that differentiate through honesty. [web:9]

4. **Multiple conversion paths** — Each service has its own CTA, plus a general "not sure" section for visitors who need guidance. This reduces friction and supports different decision-making styles. [web:18]

5. **Clear visual hierarchy** — Services are grouped logically (development, design, content, support) with consistent spacing and typography. This follows the visual hierarchy principles from high-performing service sites. [web:18]

## Content Principles Applied

This spec incorporates lessons from high-performing service pages:

1. **Lead with outcomes, not deliverables** — Following the pattern from companies like Ramp ("save an average of 5% on spending") and Painter Bros (bold, outcome-driven messaging). [web:9][web:18]

2. **Structure content to build confidence** — Each service card answers the key questions: What do I get? How does it work? What's next? This mirrors the approach at Nitsch Engineering and Millhouse Inc. [web:18]

3. **Use specific, scannable language** — Bullet points are short and concrete, not vague marketing speak. This follows the clarity principles from tech companies like Vercel and Linear. [web:9]

4. **Embed trust signals naturally** — The "Why This Matters" section builds credibility through honesty about limitations — a pattern used by companies like Anthropic that differentiate through restraint. [web:9]

5. **Design for multiple personas** — The service grid supports both visitors who know what they need (scan and click) and those who need guidance ("Not Sure" section). This mirrors the flexible entry points at companies like Retool and PostHog. [web:9][web:18]
